## Stable Principles

Classical writing quality. These rules apply regardless of how AI writing patterns, search algorithms, or best practices evolve.

### Voice and tone

- Write as a knowledgeable human practitioner: direct, specific, authoritative. *(Source: `_GLOBAL_RULES`)*
- Address the reader as "you" — not "businesses", "organizations", or "users". *(Source: `_GLOBAL_RULES`)*
- Have opinions. Do not merely report facts neutrally — react to them, take a perspective, demonstrate genuine expertise. *(Source: seo-editor)*
- First-person perspective is appropriate when it fits context — "we" for business writing, "I" for editorial or personal content. *(Sources: seo-editor, humanizer)*

### Sentence variety and rhythm

- Vary sentence length. Mix short, punchy sentences (8–15 words) with longer, more developed ones (25–35 words). Do not write consecutive paragraphs of uniform sentence length. *(Sources: `_GLOBAL_RULES`, humanizer)*
- Vary paragraph format: alternate between prose paragraphs, bullet lists, numbered steps, comparison tables, and callouts where appropriate. Do not default to a single format throughout a piece. *(Source: `_GLOBAL_RULES`)*

### Language economy

Reduce filler language. The following constructions add length without adding meaning. Suggested rewrites:

- "In order to" → "To"
- "Due to the fact that" → "Because"
- "At this point in time" → "Now"
- "In the event that" → "If"
- "The system has the ability to" → "The system can"
- "It is important to note that" → Delete. If the point is worth making, make it directly.

*(Sources: seo-editor, humanizer)*

### Active voice

Prefer active voice. Name the actor. *(Sources: seo-editor, content-strategy, humanizer)*

Before: "Reports are generated automatically."  
After: "The system generates reports automatically."

### Specificity and substance

- Prefer specific, concrete statements over vague generalizations. *(Source: content-strategy)*
- Do not pad content with vague advice. Every sentence should carry meaning. *(Source: seo-article-writer)*
- Avoid generic positive conclusions ("The future looks bright", "Exciting times lie ahead"). End with a specific, concrete next step instead. *(Sources: seo-editor, humanizer)*

### Em dash discipline

Avoid em dash (—) overuse. When an em dash introduces a clause, a comma, period, or parentheses often reads more naturally. *(Sources: seo-editor, humanizer)*

## Current Best Practices

These rules address writing patterns that are currently prevalent in AI-generated text. The underlying goal — write naturally and directly — is stable. The specific vocabulary list and structural patterns may need updating as the AI writing landscape evolves. Review this section periodically.

### AI-Overused Vocabulary

The following words and phrases are currently overused by LLMs and tend to signal AI-generated writing. Avoid them where a simpler or more direct option fits naturally. **The alternatives listed are examples, not mandatory replacements.** The goal is natural writing — choose whatever word sounds most appropriate for the sentence and context.

- **delve / delving** (e.g. examine, explore, look at)
- **leverage** as a generic verb (e.g. use, apply; reserve for financial contexts)
- **robust** as a filler descriptor — describe why something is strong or reliable instead
- **seamless / seamlessly** — describe the specific quality rather than naming it
- **showcase** as a verb (e.g. show, demonstrate)
- **foster / cultivate** (e.g. build, develop)
- **highlight** as a verb in body text (e.g. note, explain, show)
- **testament** (e.g. proof, evidence)
- **pivotal, crucial, vital** as generic intensifiers — try "key" or "important", or cut the intensifier if the claim stands without it
- **underscore** as a verb (e.g. show, confirm, demonstrate)
- **garner** (e.g. earn, receive)
- **boasts** (e.g. has, includes)
- **encompasses** (e.g. includes, covers)
- **cutting-edge** (e.g. advanced, modern, latest)
- **transformative** as a filler adjective — if used, use at most once per article and only when the transformation is substantiated
- **vibrant** (e.g. busy, active, lively)
- **game-changer** — describe what changed and why instead
- **paradigm shift** (e.g. major change; or describe the shift concretely)
- **groundbreaking** (e.g. new, first, original)
- **nestled** (e.g. located, sits)
- **holistic** (e.g. complete, full)
- **synergy** (e.g. collaboration; or describe the specific relationship)
- **innovative** as an adjective — let the work speak; delete if the sentence works without it
- **it's worth noting** — make the point directly
- **in today's fast-paced world** — start with the actual point
- **in the realm of** (e.g. in, within)
- **at the end of the day** — make the point without it
- **tapestry** used metaphorically — describe the thing directly
- **shed light on** (e.g. explain, show)
- **utilize** (e.g. use)

*(Sources: `_GLOBAL_RULES`, seo-editor, humanizer)*

### Forbidden openers and closers

- Do not start a paragraph with: "Furthermore,", "Moreover,", "Additionally,". *(Sources: `_GLOBAL_RULES`, seo-article-writer)*
- Do not start a section with signposting announcements: "Let's dive in", "let's explore", "let's break this down", "here's what you need to know", "without further ado". *(Source: humanizer)*
- Do not close with vague upbeat endings: "in conclusion, it's clear that…", "The future looks bright." *(Sources: seo-article-writer, humanizer)*
- Avoid persuasive authority tropes that add ceremony without substance: "The real question is", "at its core", "what really matters is", "fundamentally", "the heart of the matter". *(Source: humanizer)*

### Structural AI patterns

These constructions are common in AI-generated text and read as formulaic. *(Source: humanizer)*

- **Negative parallelisms** — Avoid "Not only X but also Y" and "It's not just about X, it's about Y" overuse. Rewrite directly.
- **Rule of three overuse** — Avoid defaulting to three-item lists as a universal structure. Group by the natural number of items.
- **Synonym cycling (elegant variation)** — Do not rotate synonyms to avoid repeating a word. Use the same word consistently; repetition is usually less noticeable than strained alternatives.
- **False ranges** — Avoid "5–7 ways" or "3–5 benefits" where the range is not meaningful.
- **Excessive hedging** — Avoid over-qualifying statements. "The policy may affect outcomes" not "It could potentially possibly be argued that the policy might have some effect."
- **Hyphenated word pair overuse** — Reduce unnecessary hyphenation of common pairs: data-driven, decision-making, client-facing, end-to-end, high-quality, real-time. Hyphenate only when it prevents ambiguity.
- **Overuse of boldface** — Bold only genuinely critical terms. Do not bold phrases for visual texture.
- **Inline-header vertical lists** — Avoid lists where each item starts with a bolded phrase followed by a colon. Integrate into prose or use proper headings.
- **Signposting announcements** — Do not announce what you are about to say. Say it.
- **Fragmented headers** — Avoid headings followed by a sentence that simply restates the heading. Start immediately with real content.

### Copula avoidance

LLMs often substitute elaborate constructions for simple "is" / "are" / "has". Use the simpler form where it fits. *(Source: humanizer)*

Before: "Gallery 825 serves as LAAA's exhibition space."  
After: "Gallery 825 is LAAA's exhibition space."

## Platform Conventions

These rules reflect SEO OS's publishing context: professional SEO content for WordPress. They could change if the platform or content category changes.

- **Sentence case in headings** — Use sentence case, not title case. ("Strategic negotiations and global partnerships" not "Strategic Negotiations And Global Partnerships") *(Source: humanizer)*
- **No emojis in headings or bullet points** — Remove emojis from professional SEO content. *(Source: humanizer)*
